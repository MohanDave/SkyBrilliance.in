Where agentic AI is heading

The categories forming around us, in plain terms rather than jargon.

Agentic AI moved from experiments to production systems this year. Four shifts matter most for a business like yours, and they shape why we built Sentinel the way we did.

Agentic customer experience

Agents that act, not just answer

Support and routing tools are moving past scripted chat. Newer systems can notice an issue, take a first step to resolve it, and hand off to a person only when needed.

Autonomous operations monitoring

Watching the business, continuously

Instead of a monthly report, agents now watch metrics day to day, flag what changed, and adjust workflows in real time. This is the category Sentinel sits in.

Forecasting and demand sensing

Predicting what happens next

Agentic workflows are being used for demand forecasting and personalization at a level that used to require a dedicated data team, now within reach of smaller businesses.

Agent-native products

Built agent-first, not bolted on

A new wave of companies are designing products around autonomous agents from day one, rather than adding an AI feature to an existing tool. We're building Sentinel in that spirit.

Worth saying plainly: oversight has not kept pace with adoption. Only about one in five companies currently have a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents. That gap is exactly why we keep a human reviewing every output Sentinel produces, rather than letting the agent run unchecked.

Sentinel: agentic analytics, built to be checked

Our first product. An agent pipeline that watches business metrics and explains what changed, without asking you to trust a black box.

Most "AI analytics" tools run one model over your data and hand you a paragraph. We split the job into three agents with three different jobs, so no single model is responsible for both deciding what's true and deciding how to say it. The anomaly detection itself doesn't use an LLM at all. It's plain rolling-mean and standard-deviation statistics, the same math an analyst would run by hand. We only bring in Claude where language understanding actually helps: cleaning messy input and writing the explanation.

Agent 1 LLM

Clean & sanity-check

Takes a raw CSV of business metrics, catches formatting issues, missing fields, and obvious data errors before anything downstream sees it.

Agent 2 Deterministic

Detect the anomaly

Rolling mean and standard-deviation analysis, no model involved. Same input always produces the same flagged anomalies, fully auditable.

Agent 3 LLM

Narrate the finding

Turns a statistical flag into a plain-English explanation of what moved, by how much, and why it's worth a look.

Architecture, not a wrapper

Sentinel isn't a prompt wrapped around a chart. The detection layer is code you could read line by line, the kind of thing a technical buyer can actually audit instead of taking on faith.

Built for the unsupported middle

Enterprise anomaly-detection platforms assume a data team to configure and interpret them. Sentinel is aimed at the business that has metrics but no analyst, a gap most of the category still prices past.

Said plainly, held to honestly

Sentinel is in development, not in market. We'd rather tell you what it does today than what it will do eventually. That discipline is the whole point of building this in the open.

Where Sentinel sits

An honest map of the category, not a claim of endorsement from anyone in it.

The AI-analytics space is crowded and moving fast. Most of it splits into three groups. We're naming them so you can place us accurately — none of the companies below are clients, partners, or affiliated with SkyBrilliance.

Enterprise BI + AI layer
Tableau Pulse, Domo, ThoughtSpot, Sisense
Mature BI platforms that bolted anomaly detection and natural-language querying onto existing dashboards. Built for teams that already run a BI stack and a data function to maintain it.
AI-native insight tools
Outlier AI, Fabi.ai, Kissmetrics
Newer, born-AI products that push proactive anomaly alerts and plain-language explanations. Closest in spirit to Sentinel, generally priced and positioned for teams with real data volume and an existing analytics habit.
Data observability
Monte Carlo, Anomalo, Metaplane
Built for data engineering teams monitoring pipeline health, not for a founder or ops lead reading business metrics directly. Different buyer, adjacent technique.

Where that leaves Sentinel: almost everything above assumes a data team sits between the tool and the decision. We're building for the business that doesn't have one — deterministic detection you can audit, explanations in plain language, and honest labeling of what's shipped versus what's still in development.
